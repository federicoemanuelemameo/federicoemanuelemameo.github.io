Self-initiated exercise. This project is not affiliated with, commissioned by or endorsed by Servizi Comunali S.p.A.: their public app and website are used as the subject of an independent UX study.

Abstract

Rumentologo is the service people use to find out where each object goes when they sort their waste, and when they have to bring it out. It covers 122 municipalities in Lombardy, more than 500,000 inhabitants, and every evening it is asked one single question: what do I put out tonight? Today that answer is buried under six identical tiles.

This project is a full redesign of the app and of the website: search, results, feedback and interface rebuilt so the answer arrives in seconds, with no ambiguity, and so that the most useful moment happens without even opening the app.

The system

The challenge

Redesign the experience and the interfaces of an Italian waste collection service, making it searchable, accessible and consistent across devices. The territory is already at 74.4% separate collection, so the goal is not conversion: it is everyday precision. Fewer sorting mistakes, fewer missed exposures.

And the mistakes are measurable: 22–25% of foreign matter in the plastic and metals stream, against 1–3% in paper and glass.

The solution

Every waste service is built around the truck: it publishes the day of the pickup and leaves the rest of the reasoning to you. The calendar is correct and the app is useless, because it answers a question nobody asks at 21:00 on a Sunday.

So I started from the only thing the person actually does: putting the bag outside the door. The app no longer reports the pickup day: it names the evening you act, and the window to do it in. The truck becomes an implementation detail, and the mental arithmetic disappears.

  1. 1

    The headline is DOMANI: the day the lorry passes. Acting is tonight, and working that out is left as a secondary task. Match with the real world

  2. 2

    The one thing to do (bags out between 21:00 and 06:00) is the smallest, greyest text on the screen, and the last you reach. Visibility of status

  3. 3

    An OK button that confirms nothing: the screen is a notice, not a task you can close. Consistency and standards

The action and its window take the top of the screen and become the headline; the fractions move underneath as the detail of that action, and the truck's day disappears into the week strip.

Four choices

Four design choices guide the experience, based on a user-centred approach: set up once, quickly; answer before navigating; the calendar in one glance; every object gets a complete answer.

Set up once quickly

Easy and fast setup for the user, ensuring accessibility and efficiency.

Answer before navigating

Not a menu, an answer: "Tonight, plastic and organic, from 21:00". And when there is nothing to put out, it says that too.

The calendar in one glance

A month at a glance, each day dotted with its waste typology colours.

Every object gets a complete answer

Not just the typology: how to prepare it, when the next pickup is, and where to take it if it belongs to the collection centre.

Not only an app

A public service cannot assume a smartphone. The same system had to answer the person who checks the calendar from a laptop or any other device, and it had to answer the same way.

So the web is not a brochure with a download button at the bottom: it carries the whole service on the same identity, the same wording and the same four principles, re-laid out for a wider screen. What changes between devices is the format, never the answer.

Heuristic audit

Heuristic evaluation on the real screens: 8 app screens and 11 website screens, same municipality (Cesate).

A1
Home

Six tiles with the same weight, then two thirds of empty screen. The home dispatches, it never answers.

  1. 1

    "Scegli il tuo comune", used once in a lifetime, weighs exactly as much as "Prossima raccolta", which is 90% of the visits. Visibility of status

  2. 2

    Two thirds of the screen hold no information. This is where tonight's answer could sit, with no taps at all. Efficiency of use

  3. 3

    The municipal crest as a raster logo, and no persistent navigation: every move goes back through the home or the menu. Control and freedom

A2
Calendar

An infinite list, one day per row, empty days included. Today is not marked anywhere.

  1. 1

    "Primo / Secondo Semestre" is the logic the authority prints its PDFs with, not the way a person thinks about time. The unit is the month. User language

  2. 2

    A free-text "Cerca Data" field to navigate dates, where a month picker would do. Consistency and standards

  3. 3

    Empty days take a full row each: about thirty screens of scrolling per semester, and the weekly rhythm stays invisible. Efficiency of use

A3
Waste dictionary

A database exposed as it is: thousands of alphabetical entries, and a coloured label as the answer.

  1. 1

    "Centro di raccolta" is the whole answer: it does not say where the centre is, whether it is open, or how to prepare the object. The task ends outside the app. Help and documentation

  2. 2

    "Accendini" and "Accendino", "Abbigliamento inutilizzabile" and "Abiti inutilizzabili": the same object listed twice, one row apart. Consistency and standards

  3. 3

    The examples in brackets run to three lines per result; and without fuzzy search, one typo returns nothing at all. Error prevention

A4
Collection centre

Four columns of numbers with no headers, and the real question - is it open right now? - is never answered.

  1. 1

    A full-width yellow alert for a notice that only concerns non-domestic users: an alarm wasted, and therefore ignored. Error prevention

  2. 2

    "Indicazioni stradali" hands the user to an external app without ever having shown where the centre is. Control

  3. 3

    "Domenica 00:00 00:00" to say closed: the raw database value, printed as it is. User language

A5
Municipality and zone

The first step, and the most fragile: the whole calendar depends on it.

  1. 1

    Search by name only. There is no "use my location", where that would be the fastest possible answer. Efficiency of use

  2. 2

    Hundreds of municipalities in one alphabetical list, with no address and no map: the zone cannot be verified, so a wrong calendar arrives unnoticed. Error prevention

  3. 3

    Grey text on light green, below the AA contrast threshold required of a public service. Accessibility

A7
Website

The app enlarged, not a website. All the room a desktop offers goes to decoration, and the calendar is still a list you scroll.

  1. 1

    Five tiles in one narrow column in the middle of a 1900-pixel window, all the same weight. None of them is tonight's answer. Efficiency of use

  2. 2

    The decoration takes more room than the service: two thirds of the viewport are hills. Aesthetic and minimalist design

  3. 3

    A raster municipal crest and a second typeface, neither of them the app's: one service, two identities. Consistency and standards

  4. 4

    "Primo / Secondo Semestre" is the authority's filing logic printed on the screen. Nobody plans their week in semesters. User language

  5. 5

    The real answer is a PDF to download and read elsewhere: the site hands the work back. Flexibility and efficiency

  6. 6

    One row per day at 150 pixels, empty days included, with the fractions as raster badges. The weekly rhythm never becomes visible. Recognition rather than recall

Identity

A faithful restyling: the lime green and the national waste typology colours stay. What changes is how they are used — lime stops being text, where it never passed contrast, and becomes an accent.

Visual identity

The operator's logo is a field of rotated squares. Rumentologo keeps that geometry and rebuilds it at 60°: three triangles held inside a recycling loop, and the same triangles enlarged into the prism that fills the dark screens. Nothing round is allowed in the motif.
