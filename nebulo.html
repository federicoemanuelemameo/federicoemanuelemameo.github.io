Abstract

Nebulo is a multimodal product designed for asthmatic children that transforms health and environmental data into stories. By monitoring key parameters throughout the day, it fosters awareness of the connection between personal well-being and environmental conditions.

The challenge

Design a multimodal experience with intelligent voice assistants in the healthcare field.

Focus on helping people understand their body data, building trust and transparency in conversational health interfaces, and exploring ethical impacts of these technologies.

Use a hands-on UX approach to design solutions that support health literacy and well-being in everyday life.

Nebulo is a multimodal system composed by:


(a) a portable companion that accompanies the child, monitoring the environments they frequent.


(b) a dock that monitors home air quality, ensuring that the child lives in a safe environment.


(c) an app that collects the data gathered during the day, processes it, and provides understandable visualizations.

(a)

(b)

In the morning, the child lifts Nebulo from its base and wakes it up by blowing on it. Nebulo initiates a brief voice interaction, recounting its dream and asking the child about their sleep.




In the morning

Nebulo is designed to be carried by the child during his daily life in order to detect the air quality in the different environments the child visits, transforming them in planets to be visited.

During the day

At the end of each day, Nebulo tells the child a personalized story inspired by the places they explored, like a spaceship returning to its base after a day of cosmic explorations.

It recalls past journeys, allowing each new story to reference earlier adventures and create a sense of continuity.


During the night, Nebulo listens and records the quality of the child's sleep via the microphone, keeping track of it over time.



In the evening

A drawing generator based on a morphed matrix layout was developed.

From the caregiver’s perspective, the Nebulo app transforms personal and environmental data into clear, accessible information. It helps caregivers stay constantly updated, offering both a weekly overview and detailed charts for deeper insights into the child’s condition and the surrounding air quality.


Additionally, during medical visits, this data can be easily shared with healthcare professionals, supporting more informed discussions and better care decisions.

The prototype uses an Arduino Nano ESP32 with a GC9A01 round LCD TFT screen. Ideally, audio recordings and animations would be AI-generated from conversation context and user input, but due to time limits we built a scripted demo showing the agent’s personality and interaction.


User testing was conducted with a 5-year-old child. She first interacted with the aesthetic prototype to evaluate size, grip, and ergonomics, then with the larger functional prototype to explore its interactive features. We observed how the stories kept her attention throughout, while the moving and glowing light animations added a touch of magic to the experience.
