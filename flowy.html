Abstract

flowy is an AI-powered yoga instructor that enhances your yoga practice by analyzing your poses in real time and providing personalized suggestions for improvement.

For each pose, it assigns a score based on the accuracy of your execution, using three precision levels (10, 50 or 100) which represent the percentage of pose accuracy.

The challenge

'Things That Think': prototype an interaction leveraging large language models, agents or machine learning techniques. This entailed exploring creative methods for utilizing inputs such as cameras and microphones to detect events, body positions, speech and sounds, and driving various forms of output: web interfaces, images, sound, text, speech or even physical interactions.

Idle state

The solution

An object designed to assist individuals practicing yoga at home while offering real-time feedback. We envisioned a compact device that could function either as a smartphone accessory (app + phone stand) or as a standalone unit with its own screen. In both cases, portability was key.

The primary input is the front-facing camera, which tracks the user's movements. The outputs include both visual elements (a timer, the pose illustration and the performance score) and auditory feedback to guide the user in real time: flowy detects a person and activates automatically, gives vocal feedback and a score for each pose, and closes the session with a congratulatory message.

Product design

The 3D model was created in Fusion 360, featuring a custom-designed structure to securely hold the phone, and was subsequently 3D printed. The shape was inspired by the starting yoga pose: the crossed legs serve as a support base and the hands joined above the head act as a handle, making the object more manageable and portable.

User interface design

The interface is designed to be as simple and minimal as possible, ensuring that key information remains clear and easily visible from about 2.5 meters, where the user typically stands. It displays three pieces of information: a circular timer at the top, counting down pose preparation and pose holding; the pose illustration at the center; a score bar at the bottom, filling with red, yellow or green for the three performance levels.

Machine learning & code

We explored different technologies that could support our goal. After experimenting with different tools and models (Teachable Machine, LLaVA), we discovered that GPT-4o was effective enough for our needs; we also considered skeleton detection with MediaPipe, but GPT-4o proved highly reliable in identifying yoga poses.

The main logic lives in two functions: personDetection() captures a frame from the webcam and asks the model whether there is a person; poseEvaluation() asks it to judge whether the specified pose is performed correctly and returns detailed feedback with a score of 10, 50 or 100.

Round, playful shapes for the poses, to align the illustrations with the overall branding.
